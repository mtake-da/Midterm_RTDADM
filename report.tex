\section{Introduction}

For this midterm project in Real-Time Data Analysis and Decision Making (RTDADM), I built a real-time stream processing and monitoring system for an academic classroom at Astana IT University (Room~C1.3.101). The system builds upon the classroom IoT sensor dataset from Assignment~2, scaling and streaming the observations over an extended 60-minute lecture session.

The primary objective is to ingest sensor packets sequentially one minute at a time, apply online imputation for missing sensor telemetry without look-ahead bias, compute rolling statistics and physical domain indicators via sliding buffers, and detect multi-tier environmental and operational anomalies in real time.

% ============================================================
\section{Data Scenario and Stream Architecture}

\subsection{The Room and Sensor Setup}

Room~C1.3.101 is a lecture and lab space at AITU with a nominal maximum capacity of 40 students. The room is instrumented with four physical sensors:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temperature Sensor} (\texttt{Temp\_C}): Measures indoor ambient air temperature in °C.
  \item \textbf{CO\textsubscript{2} Sensor} (\texttt{CO2\_ppm}): Measures indoor carbon dioxide concentration in parts per million (ppm).
  \item \textbf{Occupancy Camera} (\texttt{Occupancy}): Provides computer-vision headcount counts of people present.
  \item \textbf{Smart Power Meter} (\texttt{Power\_kW}): Measures aggregate electrical demand of lighting, HVAC terminals, and workstation rigs in kilowatts (kW).
\end{itemize}

Incoming sensor packets arrive at 1-minute intervals. The stream simulation uses a fixed pseudo-random seed ($42$) to guarantee strict determinism and reproducibility.

\subsection{Lecture Simulation Phases}

The 60-minute timeline is divided into six operational lecture phases:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{10:00--10:11 (Assignment 2 Baseline):} 12 historical records containing 3 intentional sensor dropouts at minutes 10:02, 10:04, and 10:07.
  \item \textbf{10:12--10:25 (Full Lecture Capacity):} Class reaches full occupancy ($35$--$40$ students); steady environmental conditions.
  \item \textbf{10:26--10:35 (Ventilation Failure):} Rapid $\text{CO}_2$ buildup, crossing the 1000 ppm warning threshold and surging into critical levels ($>1200$ ppm).
  \item \textbf{10:36--10:45 (Thermal Accumulation):} Ambient heat accumulates steadily under full attendance, exceeding the $24.0$°C comfort boundary.
  \item \textbf{10:46--10:50 (Laboratory Practical \& Power Surge):} Intensive student computing workstations activate, pushing electrical load above the $6.0$ kW threshold.
  \item \textbf{10:51--10:59 (Classroom Dismissal):} Students depart ($|\Delta\text{Occupancy}| \ge 6$), ventilation recovers, and power returns to idle.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implementation}
\subsection{Stream Generator (\texttt{generate\_stream})}

The \texttt{generate\_stream(total\_records=60, seed=42)} function initializes the 12 empirical records from Assignment~2 and iteratively simulates the remaining 48 minutes according to the lecture timeline. Gaussian noise ($\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma)$) models physical variation and sensor jitter. In addition to the 3 baseline dropouts (10:02, 10:04, 10:07), three realistic transmission dropouts are injected into the extended stream:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{10:18}: \texttt{Temp\_C} is missing (\texttt{None})
  \item \textbf{10:33}: \texttt{CO2\_ppm} is missing (\texttt{None})
  \item \textbf{10:44}: \texttt{Occupancy} is missing (\texttt{None})
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stream Processor Architecture (\texttt{StreamProcessor})}

The streaming core is encapsulated in the \texttt{StreamProcessor} class. Records are ingested one by one via \texttt{process(record)} with zero look-ahead capability, maintaining state in sliding ring buffers (\texttt{collections.deque(maxlen=5)}):

\subsubsection{Step 1: Online Imputation}

Missing sensor fields are imputed dynamically prior to indicator calculation:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Continuous Sensors (\texttt{Temp\_C}, \texttt{CO2\_ppm}):} When at least two previous valid values exist in the buffer ($x_{t-2}, x_{t-1}$), the missing value is predicted using linear trend extrapolation:
  \begin{equation}
    \hat{x}_t = \frac{x_{t-2} + x_{t-1}}{2} + (x_{t-1} - x_{t-2}) = x_{t-1} + \frac{x_{t-1} - x_{t-2}}{2}
  \end{equation}
  If insufficient history is present in the buffer, the engine falls back to the last known state. For instance, at 10:02 the imputed temperature of $21.80$°C smoothly bridges $21.5$°C and $21.7$°C, and at 10:04 the imputed $\text{CO}_2$ concentration of $735.0$ ppm smoothly extrapolates the observed trend.
  \item \textbf{Discrete Sensors (\texttt{Occupancy}):} Forward-fills the most recent valid headcount.
\end{itemize}
Whenever an imputation occurs, the event is immediately flagged with a \texttt{SENSOR\_DROPOUT} alert.

\subsubsection{Step 2: Real-Time Indicator Engineering}

Using sliding buffers of length $W=5$, the engine derives multiple rolling metrics:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Rolling Moving Averages:} Dampens minute-by-minute measurement noise:
  \begin{equation}
    \bar{T}_t = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} T_{t-i}, \quad
    \bar{C}_t = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} C_{t-i}, \quad
    \bar{P}_t = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} P_{t-i} \quad (N \le 5)
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Rolling Volatility (Standard Deviation):} Tracks volatility in temperature and electrical demand:
  \begin{equation}
    \sigma_T^{(t)} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} (T_{t-i} - \bar{T}_t)^2}, \quad
    \sigma_P^{(t)} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-1} (P_{t-i} - \bar{P}_t)^2}
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Temperature Velocity (Delta):} Measures sudden thermal shift: $\Delta T_t = T_t - T_{t-1}$.
  \item \textbf{Domain Composite Indicators:}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Occupancy Density:} $d_t = \text{Occupancy}_t / 40.0$ (proportion of nominal room capacity).
    \item \textbf{Specific Energy per Occupant:} $e_t = P_t / \max(\text{Occupancy}_t, 1)$ (kW per student).
    \item \textbf{Comfort Compliance Boolean:} $\text{comfort\_ok}_t \iff (20.0 \le T_t \le 24.0) \land (C_t \le 1000.0)$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Step 4: Stream Ingestion and Event Structuring}

Each processed record yields an enriched record dictionary (16 attributes) appended to \texttt{proc.history}, and every triggered alert appends an incident entry to \texttt{proc.anomalies}. Converted into pandas DataFrames (\texttt{df} and \texttt{df\_anom}), these structures provide full auditability and feed the visualization suite without any batch-mode lookahead bias.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{System Strengths and Observations}

\textbf{Zero Lookahead Integrity:} Unlike batch analytics where global imputation and global windowing can accidentally introduce future leakage, the stream processing engine operates strictly on current and historical records.

\textbf{Smooth Imputation via Buffer Trend Extrapolation:} When the temperature sensor dropped out at 10:02, the trend extrapolation $\hat{T}_{10:02} = 21.80$°C seamlessly continued the rising gradient ($21.5$°C $\to$ $21.7$°C), preserving downstream rolling statistics without discontinuity. Similarly, the missing $\text{CO}_2$ reading at 10:04 was smoothly interpolated to $735.0$ ppm.

\textbf{Compound Multi-Sensor Incident Detection:} The system excelled at capturing overlapping failures. Between 10:32 and 10:35, the classroom reached an acute risk state where poor indoor air quality, thermal discomfort, and heavy workstation electrical demand converged simultaneously.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}

While the in-memory simulation accurately models sensor dynamics, industrial deployment would incorporate:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Message Broker Integration:} Connecting to Apache Kafka or MQTT brokers to ingest real physical IoT streams.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive Dynamic Baselines:} Replacing static thresholds with rolling $z$-scores ($\mu \pm 3\sigma$) or contextual thresholds conditioned on class schedule and external weather.
  \item \textbf{Automated Actuation:} Feeding alerts into Building Management Systems (BMS) to automatically cycle HVAC fans when $\text{CO}_2$ exceeds 1000 ppm.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

This midterm project successfully designed and implemented an end-to-end real-time stream processing system for classroom monitoring. By taking raw sensor records one-by-one, handling dropouts via online extrapolation, calculating sliding-window indicators, and triggering multi-tier anomaly alarms, the architecture satisfies all requirements of modern stream computing.

The project underscores key differences between streaming and batch paradigms: real-time processing mandates strict state management, ring buffer discipline, and causal time guarantees.
